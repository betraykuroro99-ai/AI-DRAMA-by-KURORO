# Script review and revision checklist

For each issue, identify the problem, cite the relevant beat/scene/line, explain its effect on understanding, emotion or pacing, and propose a concrete fix. Order issues by impact. List effective elements worth preserving only when they are genuinely useful.

- [ ] Premise, genre and intended audience align; the opening establishes the genre promise.
- [ ] The story and every episode have a goal, obstacle, turn and outcome.
- [ ] Episode content fits the requested duration without repetition or stalled scenes.
- [ ] Choices follow established goals, pressures or new events.
- [ ] Character change is supported by visible actions and costs.
- [ ] Each scene changes information, relationships, goals, risk or emotion.
- [ ] Conflict escalates through events rather than explanation alone.
- [ ] Dialogue fits the relationships and immediate objectives.
- [ ] Actions, reactions, avoidance and testing carry important information.
- [ ] Twists have prior clues and coherent causality.
- [ ] Episode hooks create a new question, risk or irreversible cost.
- [ ] Cast, locations, props and actions fit the production budget and constraints.
- [ ] Actions can be seen or heard without inaccessible inner explanation.
- [ ] Episode titles, scene headings, character labels and on-screen messages are consistent.
- [ ] Standard-format scene headings include number, INT./EXT., location and time.
- [ ] Revisions fix comprehension, emotion and pacing while preserving effective material.
