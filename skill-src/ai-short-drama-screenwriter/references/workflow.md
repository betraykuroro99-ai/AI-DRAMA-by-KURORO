# Short-drama writing workflow

For a full project, establish a minimal brief: genre, premise, protagonist's goal, main obstacle, audience, episode count, duration per episode, production constraints and deliverables. Mark reasonable assumptions. Do not force romance, melodrama or a fixed episode count.

## 1. Creative brief

Identify immutable constraints and missing details that materially affect direction. Deliver a brief that can be traced through the final script.

## 2. Premise and central conflict

Connect the protagonist's desire, obstacle and audience expectations. Deliver a premise and one-sentence hook that can generate choices and consequences within the production limits.

## 3. Series and episode structure

Plan goals, obstacles, turns and outcomes for the full story and each episode. Deliver dramatic beats and opening or closing hooks. Each episode must add irreversible information, a decision or a cost; avoid repetition as padding.

## 4. Characters and relationships

Establish each character's goal, fear, secret, resources and relationships. Make needs conflict. Deliver compact profiles and the choices that drive change. Support every change with prior events, visible actions or accumulated costs.

## 5. Scenes and escalating conflict

For each scene establish the location, time, immediate goal, obstacle, change in strategy and new ending state. Every scene changes information, relationships, goals, risk or emotion.

## 6. Dialogue and subtext

Use avoidance, testing, interruption and physical reactions to convey unspoken needs. Match the character's voice and immediate purpose. For dialogue-only edits, deliver the revised lines directly, with a minimal adjacent acting cue if useful. Do not rebuild the entire story.

## 7. Emotion, suspense, humor, twists and hooks

Place clues before reveals. Build emotion through choices and their consequences. A twist must remain credible on a second viewing; a closing hook must create a specific next question or cost, rather than merely interrupting an event.

## 8. Draft and revision

Write visible, audible actions in the present tense. Match the requested script or outline format. Revise comprehension, emotional progression and pacing first. Preserve effective material and production constraints. For mixed requests, deliver the script before the production storyboard.

## Production handoff

After approval, preserve the final script in the project source folder. Register character, location, prop and voice references, then map each approved line and action to timed shots using `SPEC.md`. Estimate Tagalog or English speech by words and a timed read, not the upstream Chinese character count.
