# Short-drama script format

## Episode heading

Use `Episode N — Title` when relevant. Do not invent a full season's titles unless requested.

## Scene headings

Every scene uses a scene number, INT./EXT., location and time, for example:

`1. INT. CONVENIENCE STORE — NIGHT`

For standard or vertical short-drama format, all four fields are required. A generic cast/location list does not replace scene headings.

## Action and dialogue

Write present, visible and audible actions. Put each character's name on its own line and dialogue beneath it. Use only short necessary acting cues; do not use parentheticals to explain inaccessible inner thoughts. Dialogue should change the conflict or relationship rather than merely explain backstory.

Mark off-screen speech as `NAME (OS)` and narration or inner voice as `NAME (VO)`. Introduce visible messages explicitly, such as `PHONE SCREEN —` or `ON-SCREEN TEXT:`. Clearly mark flashbacks and return to the present with an action. Use a short transition such as `Cut to black` when needed.

For requested vertical video, keep essential faces, information and actions legible in a single vertical composition. Do not add lens sizes, camera numbers or model parameters unless requested.

## Example

```text
Episode 1

1. INT. CONVENIENCE STORE — NIGHT

Rain streaks the glass door. Lina stacks a shelf. Her phone vibrates twice.

PHONE SCREEN — A number inactive for three years: "Don't look for me."

Lina looks up. A red umbrella stops outside the door.

LINA
Mom?

The figure outside turns away. Lina runs to the door.
The telephone on the counter rings.

Cut to black.
```
