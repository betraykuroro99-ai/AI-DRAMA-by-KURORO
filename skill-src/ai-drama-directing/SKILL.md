# AI Drama Directing

Use for storyboards, video prompts and editorial pacing. Follow the approved script and the project's actual language, style, format and budget. Read SPEC.md for production mechanics.

## Episode flow

Open with an immediate visual or dialogue hook, normally within the first 3–5 seconds. Establish a concrete question, disruption or consequence. Progress through actions, discoveries, decisions and changing relationships. End the story on a meaningful cliffhanger, then use the ending skill for ABANGAN. Do not reveal later surprises in cover copy.

Every shot must advance plot, reveal character, establish essential geography, plant/pay off a clue or change the emotional stakes. Record its purpose in the shot plan. Remove redundant establishing shots, repetitive reactions, walking and empty holds. Keep pauses only when they communicate a specific emotional change. Never stretch runtime through slowed speech, slow motion, frozen frames or recycled footage. If a minimum episode duration requires more content, add consequential beats to the script for user review.

## Performance and coverage

Use natural conversational speed and restrained expressions. Prompt small jaw openings, subtle lip articulation, relaxed cheeks and stable facial proportions. Avoid exaggerated teeth exposure, broad rubbery mouth motion and repeated nodding. Use purposeful listener reactions, inserts and over-shoulder coverage where the speaker need not be visible; retain action clarity and screen direction. Never hide an essential story action merely to avoid a difficult generation.

Use short, single-purpose actions with limited simultaneous movement. Identify left/right anatomy from the character's perspective. Specify screen position separately. Keep faces stable during turns; avoid unnecessary extreme head rotations and competing actions. Negative prompts are guidance, not proof of quality; review the actual output with the visual QA skill.

For Beauty and Bagwis: Tagalog dialogue, portrait 9:16 at 480p, established illustrated character identities, two-minute minimum story duration excluding covers/ending cards. Keep English production instructions. These project choices are defaults for this series, not universal restrictions.

## Native dialogue with voice references

When the user chooses native generated speech, use neutral character voice samples for identity and exact script text for the new performance. Avoid a separate lip-sync or dubbing stage unless requested. On Venice, check `reference_audio_urls` support independently of the catalog’s single-field `audio_input` flag; see `docs/tagalog-and-wan-reference.md`. Preserve the user’s actual accepted editing and last-frame prop state. Do not overwrite their zooms, muted sections or character design with earlier project references.
